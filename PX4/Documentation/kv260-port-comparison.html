Two ports of NuttX to the KV260 RPU

A comparison of the upstream-style kv260-rpu branch with the month-long zynqmpsoc-r5 + PX4 effort, and what the second one learned on real silicon that the first still needs.

On the Six Steps plan, theirs is about five steps ahead: hardware, then DDR + MPU + caches, then RPMsg, then PX4. Ours is the one that could go upstream.

Hardware findings to carry over

These come from the other session's commits and notes, measured on real silicon. Verify each against UG1085 or the board before relying on it.

  1. UART0 is electrically dead on the KV260 Affects us
    No MIO pin, and the FSBL never enables its clock (CRL_APB_UART0_REF_CTRL, 0xFF5E0074). UART1 is the USB-UART but it is Linux's console (ttyPS1). Our defconfig puts the console on UART0, so expect no output on hardware. They kept UART0 as a dummy console and did real I/O over rpmsg.
  2. TTC ownership Affects us
    TTC0 belongs to Linux (ttc-pwm, pwmchip0); our tick uses TTC0 (zynqmp_rpu_timerisr.c:50). TTC2 and TTC3 have their clocks gated in CRL_APB and never count. TTC1 is the usable one: counter 0 for the tick, counter 1 for an HRT. A read-modify-write on CRL_APB to ungate clocks once forced a power cycle.
  3. RPU GIC state survives remoteproc stop/start Affects us
    An image killed between IAR and EOIR leaves an interrupt active, which pins RPR and blocks every later interrupt. They clear it in up_irqinitialize by walking GICD_ISACTIVER, writing EOI per set bit, then GICD_ICACTIVER. Ours does not.
  4. GIC writes do stick Skip this path
    They spent about two weeks on "discarded GIC writes" (XPPU/XMPU, security banking) and closed it as a false alarm on 2026-07-21.
  5. IPI wiring for RPMsg RPMsg step
    RPU0 channel at 0xFF310000, inbound on RPU GIC IRQ 65 (SPI 33). The kick bit is 24, the PL0 slot, because Xilinx's device tree gives remoteproc the PL0 mailbox. Answer every kick with a TRIG write, or TF-A stops re-enabling delivery on the A53 side.
  6. Caches and the MPU DDR step
    I-cache alone gained nothing measurable. The D-cache took the EKF2 update from 2533 to 500 µs and CPU load from about 95% to 40%. MPU regions must be non-shareable or the R5 treats them as uncached. The resource table, trace ring and vrings must be Strongly-Ordered, since nothing in rptun does cache maintenance.
  7. Two Kconfig traps Ours is fine
    CONFIG_RAM_SIZE is an int, so a hex value parses as 0; ours is decimal. Their TTC_INTERVAL overflowed 32 bits and ran the tick 26× fast; ours divides first and also applies the -1 for INTERVAL + 1.

Weak spots in theirs

  • Generic drivers patched under #ifdef CONFIG_ZYNQR5_RPTUN: drivers/rptun/rptun.c gains 224 lines (polling fallback, dsb everywhere), plus serial/uart_rpmsg.c, syslog_rpmsg.c and fs/rpmsgfs. Upstream would reject these. The polled IPI in up_idle is left over from the GIC false alarm and is probably unnecessary now.
  • Shared armv7-r files changed for every chip: arm_head.S force-defines CPU_DCACHE_DISABLE, HEAD_ASRC moved to CMN_ASRCS, an addrenv.h stub added. One change is worth taking: building arm_mpu.c when CONFIG_ARM_MPU is set, not only under BUILD_PROTECTED.
  • No board directory inside NuttX. The board exists only in PX4's tree, so plain NuttX has no defconfig to build.
  • No QEMU path, so every change needs the hardware to test.

Recommendation

Keep kv260-rpu as the base. Treat theirs as a reference, not a merge source.

Before the board Move the tick to TTC1 counter 0. Add the stale-active-IRQ clear. Plan for no UART0 output: use JTAG/xsdb or a memory breadcrumb until RPMsg works.
DDR, MPU, caches Reuse their split-mode layout and the MPU region list from zynqr5_start.c as hardware-proven facts.
RPMsg Port zynqr5_rsc.c and the IPI notify/ISR logic, without the rptun.c polling changes.

Housekeeping in ours: board.h still carries TMS570 leftovers (BOARD_ASWAIT/RWAIT/EWAIT, BOARD_PINMUX_INITIALIZER, the GIOA7 button, LED D11), and zynqmp_rpu_irq_delete.h is untracked.
